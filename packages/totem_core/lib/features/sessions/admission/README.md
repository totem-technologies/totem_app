# Client-side Session admission

This is cooperative client-side admission, **not server-enforced authorization**. The existing `/rooms/{session_slug}/join` endpoint records attendance when it issues a token, before the Keeper admits anyone. Its token allows connection to the actual LiveKit room. Pending clients occupy one of the room's 10 LiveKit slots, and an old or modified client can bypass this flow. The backend's speaking-order reconciliation also sees pending LiveKit connections; client presentation filtering cannot repair the server's `talking_order`. The Keeper's client avoids requesting a reconciliation solely because an unknown participant connected, but other server events can still include them. The backend remains the authority on join timing (including its earlier first-join cutoff); the UI cannot promise T+15 access.

## Connection and admission

The participant obtains one existing Session token and connects once to a normal-configured LiveKit `Room` with `autoSubscribe: false`. Pending users publish no microphone or camera tracks and have only a local pre-join preview. The request is a reliable targeted packet on `totem.admission.v1`. Its ID remains stable on presence/reconnection retransmission and expires two minutes after creation, independently of Session eligibility. On approval, admission listeners are removed and ownership of the **connected room** moves from pre-join to `SessionController.activateConnectedRoom`; no second token request, `prepareConnection`, `Room.connect`, or deliberate disconnect occurs. The Session controller registers its listeners, loads current room state/participants, starts normal infrastructure and remote subscriptions (existing and future publications), transfers and publishes the same pre-join media tracks, and only then reports activation success. It owns subsequent room teardown. Keeper direct joining retains its normal join path.

The newly activated participant sends `admission.joined` with the original request ID on the same connection. The Keeper checks the authenticated LiveKit sender, approved request, ID and expiry before marking the attempt joined. Failed activation never broadcasts admission; if the room is lost before a failure packet is possible, the Keeper reports a timeout and the participant can retry. A publish failure of the receipt is retried only on Keeper presence, hello or room reconnect, not by polling.

## Presentation roster

The admission topic also carries targeted `roster.request`, correlated `roster.snapshot`, and revisioned `roster.updated` packets. A requesting identity not yet admitted receives a correlated **empty** snapshot so its client can process a subsequent roster update without seeing existing participants while waiting; full snapshots and updates are sent only to admitted identities. The Keeper broadcasts a new roster only after validating a joined receipt. Each updated client starts with only the authoritative Keeper identity visible, asks the Keeper for a snapshot, rejects packets from non-Keepers, wrong sessions, expired or stale revisions and unmatched snapshot requests, and fails closed if its roster cannot be established. Only admitted identities are shown in participant tiles, counts, speaker/next-speaker presentation and reorder controls. The canonical backend room state and LiveKit presence are unchanged. The participant's normal Session UI stays on its loading surface until its identity is in the Keeper-announced roster.

Updated clients restrict subscriptions to their published tracks via the pinned LiveKit SDK's `setTrackSubscriptionPermissions`: Keeper media is available only to admitted identities; other admitted publishers allow the Keeper and roster members. This is cooperative and does not protect against unmodified or malicious publishers. While pending, the participant deliberately does not subscribe to remote publications; on activation it manually subscribes to existing and subsequently published tracks.

Roster state and decisions are not durable. The Keeper's in-process store preserves the admitted roster/revision/generation across a controller reattach, but a terminated Keeper process cannot reliably reconstruct past decisions or synchronize independent Keeper devices. Correlated snapshots and roster resynchronization do not turn LiveKit data packets into persistent storage; clients must fail closed when recovery is impossible. No backend room metadata or participant attributes are used as admission authority.

## Four-device smoke test and performance capture

Use a real Keeper (A), two already-admitted participants (B, C), and an applicant (D). In LiveKit/Flutter DevTools record explicit room `connect`/`disconnect` calls and Flutter frame timings around the `session_admission_room_connect` and `session_admission_room_activation` Timeline tasks, including queue-update transitions. On a successful D admission, verify one explicit connect, zero approval-time disconnects/reconnects, one room instance, and no second token request. Compare frame times against the previous build before claiming a performance gain. Verify the same preview track persists through waiting and that neither D's local media nor remote subscription is active before approval. B and C must never show D, even for one frame after D connects; D should appear on A/B/C only after Keeper processes `admission.joined` and sends a roster update. Check local media publication, audio playback, and media-track permissions after activation.

Repeat with camera off, microphone off, both off, Keeper disconnect/reconnect, applicant cancellation, simultaneous applicants, and near the 10-person room limit. Run in desktop Safari, mobile Safari, Chrome, iOS and Android where supported; verify no duplicated camera capture or Safari platform-view flicker. This checklist requires real LiveKit connections and is not proven by unit/widget tests.
